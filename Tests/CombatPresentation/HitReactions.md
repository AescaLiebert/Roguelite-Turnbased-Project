# Card execution target reactions

On a damage skill rank, ultimate level or damage counter, open **Runtime Effect → Damage Animation → Target Reaction**. Choose Hit (the existing recoil), None, Knock Back, Knock Down, or Knock Up. **Reaction On** selects Last Hit (default), First Hit or Every Hit. Non-selected hits retain light hit recoil unless the card selects None.

New **Ultimate Card** ScriptableObjects start with one C0 tier whose damage reaction is **Knock Up / Last Hit**. New `UltimateLevelData` instances use the same default. The controls remain editable: loading existing assets or custom reactions does not force them to knockup, and non-damage ultimate effects do not animate damage reactions.

- **Knock Back:** push away, hold a grounded backward-leaning posture for one second, stand at the displaced location, then run back to the original place.
- **Knock Down:** trip face down around the body's center in the same horizontal place, then get up.
- **Knock Up:** an upward impulse sends the target along an arc away from the actual attacker. The body stays mostly upright in flight, lands away from its slot, falls onto its back, gets up at the landing point, then returns smoothly to formation.
- **Stance tolerance:** a stance suppresses all hit motion, including the ordinary recoil. Damage numbers, shields, crit/block and sound still resolve. This does not reduce damage or change action legality.
- **Stance cancellation:** an explicit Remove Stance effect or Cancel Stance attack keyword that removes an active stance forces exactly one Knock Back on the removal event. It overrides the selected card reaction for that recipient for the rest of that action. A cancellation against a recipient with no stance produces no forced reaction. Cancellation in Before Action, Damaging, After Damage or After Action follows its own authored timing; a utility card can cancel a stance without a damage packet.
- **AOE:** each recipient resolves stance tolerance independently, and reactions animate together. Player and opponent recipients follow the same rules.
- **Multi-hit:** every damage packet still has independent combat rolls. Strong reactions default to the final hit, including the existing overkill sequence. A strong reaction on an earlier hit launches/displaces the target once, then holds it through the remaining hits instead of compressing, restarting and snapping home on every hit. Knockup holds at its apex; knockdown and knockback hold their displaced poses. Later light/None packets cannot interrupt that active response. Damage feedback/audio still occurs for every hit at the barrage speed. Reactions are visual, with no extra damage, stun status, card cost or formation change.

Battle events record the resolved reaction, resistance and cancellation facts before passive damage reactions run. Playback uses those facts rather than looking at the final battle's stance. Event clones, serialization and recipient projections preserve them. Older recordings use their existing recoil with stance tolerance.

## Animation and state

Entry: a damage or successful stance-removal event during card execution. Exit: the final hit releases the held response, allowing descent/fall and full recovery to the originally captured pose. If the final packet is evaded or the combo ends early, the After Damage/action-completion barrier releases any remaining holds and waits for every recipient to be ready. `TargetsReady` reports completion; `WaitForTargetReactions` releases and awaits the pending responses. Attacker recovery and the next card cannot progress while a target is still recovering. No extra combat resources are consumed during a hold.

Knockup uses a captured visible-foot anchor (renderer bounds minimum), independent of whether the prefab root is at the feet, hips or body centre. A decelerating rise leads into a short high apex, then an accelerating drop while horizontal momentum carries the actor away. Airborne tilt is limited; the full back fall happens after touchdown, not at the apex. Landing/get-up floor contact is corrected against the original visible ground height, so a centre-pivot actor does not hover or intersect the floor. Its temporary displaced position is presentation-only; formation slots and combat targeting stay unchanged.

The camera reserves the reaction envelope before entering Attack, including the apex and displaced landing when the recorded action contains knockup. Rest, Wind Up and Attack are fixed snapshots: no live caster/target tracking, impact shake, recovery reframe or extra ultimate camera shots. Attack remains fixed through target and attacker recovery. Approach and attacker return still run once per card. No reaction causes the attacker to approach again. Death remains owned by the existing post-action defeat flow, after reactions finish. Skip/disable explicitly cancels playback and restores all affected positions, facing and scales before planning. A forced override blends from the current displaced pose without replacing the original recovery anchor. This remains scripted visual movement, not a Rigidbody/collision simulation.

Optional Animator triggers: `Hurt`, `KnockBack`, `KnockDown`, `KnockUp`, `KnockFall`, `GetUp`, `Run`, `Idle`. Return also supports named Run/Idle states and legacy clips ending in `_run`/`_idle`. Procedural root animation supplies displacement even on rigs without locomotion clips; final humanoid running motion needs those clips. The reaction system does not change shared Animator assets.

Starting values on **BattleStagePresenter → Target Reactions**: knockback distance 1.6 world units; 0.2 s push, designer-adjustable 1.0 s hold, 0.15 s stand and 0.35 s return. Knockdown retains 0.65 s. Knockup duration 2.0 s, height 2.4 and away distance 1.8 world units (plus time held while hits remain). Strong motion does not scale down to a barrage interval. Light recoil lasts 0.28 s and scales with barrage speed. Knockup spends 30% rising, 10% at the apex, 22% dropping, 14% falling/grounded, 13% standing and 11% returning; airborne tilt peaks at 12 degrees. Every Hit holds at 35%, inside the apex.

Validate with small and large production models: pass if flight is visibly upward-and-away, the body stays mostly upright until touchdown, prone/get-up bounds touch the floor, return begins only after standing, and launch/landing remain inside the action shot. For Every Hit knockup, pass if the target remains airborne through a ten-hit sequence and the next card starts only after fall/get-up/return. If throw force is unclear, increase away distance before increasing tilt; if the body appears floaty, shorten flight duration or lower height; if return is rushed, increase knockup duration. Do not compress the motion into a barrage interval.

The game-design guidance influenced the default final-hit reaction, stance holding its pose, preserved damage feedback/audio, and deterministic recovery rather than a new combat disable state. The continuous multi-hit lifecycle favors readable cause/effect and action readiness over squeezing a fall/get-up into every hit. The directional arc/foot anchor makes the source of force clear, and touchdown sound separates flight from landing. Damage, action costs and balance remain unchanged.

Manual production playtest:

1. Readability/new player: compare the three strong reactions on the same fighter. Pass if their direction and outcome are distinguishable without explanation.
2. Stress: run a ten-hit AOE with one stance holder and a zero-HP recipient. Pass if the stance holder stays still, the full sequence resolves, and everyone returns or enters defeat cleanly.
3. Timing/skill: cancel a stance with a card set to Knock Up, then play another card after the cancellation. Pass if the cancel card forces Knock Back once and the later card uses its own setting.
4. Abuse/input: skip during flight, prone hold and recovery; spam card input. Pass if no duplicated damage/actions, lingering displaced actors or camera lock occurs.
5. Fit: check both teams and narrow windows on actual rigs. Pass if reactions are readable, get-up and death do not fight over the pose, and the camera contains the relevant actors.
