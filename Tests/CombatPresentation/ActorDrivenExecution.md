## October 9 type/rank camera correction (current)

There are only three camera states: overhead Rest, WindUp and Action. The latest clarification is **Buff/Heal cuts only; offensive cards move quickly and follow the attacker**. Offensive Rank 1 moves quickly behind the caster at WindUp; Rank 2/3 cuts to the face. Ranked Action first cuts back behind, then follows the attacking caster. Recovery follows the caster home to the saved rear WindUp shot, removing any ranked Action roll. Target focus, geometry and the reaction envelope are captured positions, never live target tracking; launches cannot pull the camera. AoE retains the whole opposing formation even with one survivor. Ultimate energy remains within WindUp, without extra camera states. Queue entry moves to the next offensive WindUp, except support boundaries use cuts; an empty queue returns to overhead Rest. Skip/disable cancels transition/follow and immediately restores Rest.

Every face shot now frames approximately the upper two-thirds of the model rather than a head/shoulder close-up. Higher rank and ultimate shots retain their dramatic angle/roll but use the same body coverage. Geometry-based framing supports rigs without a head bone.

Rank 2 Buff/Heal always cuts to face WindUp, for self, selected ally and whole-team scopes. At Action entry it immediately cuts to exactly the Rank 1 shot: rear caster/opposing formation for self; full allied rear-team view otherwise. That shot then holds through Action/Recovery. Rank 1 remains unchanged and skips actor WindUp. Rank 3 also keeps its previous sequence: non-self holds full allied rear-team framing; self holds face through Action and cuts behind at Recovery, after the authored Action clip and effect barriers finish. Support transitions never interpolate or insert a late Action reset.

`ActorCardPresentation` owns Rest, WindUp, Action and Recovery. `BattleStagePresenter.Execution` creates one card context with the actor, category, rank, target-position snapshot and rear wind-up shot. The camera observes actor phase changes. Playback supplies category and target scope before preparation; damage, hit and status authority remain in Core.

Battle facing is derived from the positions of the two formation centers, so identical or stale anchor rotations cannot make both teams face the same way. `BattleFighterView` uses `actor / Model yaw correction / Rig + Animator`. The correction is above the Animator binding root, so an imported clip that resets rig yaw cannot erase it. The current sample art uses a 180-degree visual yaw correction (`CharacterObject.FighterModelYawOffset`); models authored facing +Z can set it to 0. Player and enemy models therefore face each other while camera shots and recovery use the stable actor direction. Formation refresh and reserve positioning use the same rule; changing the model's animated yaw cannot reverse the camera's rear/front reference. The camera fixture tests both model conventions and both sides, including the no-anchor spawn fallback.

| Card family | Rank 1 WindUp | Rank 2/3 WindUp | Action | Recovery |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Attack, AttackDebuff, Debuff, non-self Stance | Rapid move to rear; selected opponents face caster | Face/shoulder cut | Ranked cut behind, then quick entry/caster follow | Follow caster home to saved rear WindUp |
| Self-target Stance | Rapid move to rear | Actor portrait cut | Ranked cut behind; perform stance | Return to rear WindUp |
| Self-target Buff, Recovery/heal | Skip actor WindUp; cut behind, with full opposing formation ahead | Two-thirds actor portrait cut | Rank 2 cuts to Rank 1 rear shot at entry; Rank 3 holds face | Rank 1/2 hold rear; Rank 3 cuts behind after Action ends |
| Other Buff, Recovery/heal | Skip actor WindUp; cut to rear full allied formation | Rank 2 face cut; Rank 3 team shot | Rank 2 cuts to Rank 1 team shot at entry; all ranks hold team Action | Hold team shot, then cut to next card/Rest |

Self targeting comes from `EffectTargetScope.Self` in production; standalone preview calls infer one recipient matching the source. A single selected ally remains a team-support shot. The target focus is a captured world position/bounds and does not track recoil, rotation, death or later combo target changes. AoE captures the whole opposing formation even with one survivor; damage still resolves against living recipients. Reserves enter the formation registry after landing, while defeated slots retain their geometry.

Attack composition uses a rear shoulder camera following only the caster root, with a captured target-position look point. It frames the target row fully while allowing the caster's lower body to extend into the foreground/HUD area. Rank 3 Action roll settles back to the original WindUp rotation during Recovery. Every queue-empty return uses the overhead drafting view, deliberately different from the clips' rear rest view.

- Ultimate 0–0.7 s: compact red ENEMY TURN above the hand while the arena camera changes sides. The implementation synchronizes the announcement with a polar orbit to the incoming side's overhead drafting position.

Turn announcements now use cyan/player or coral/enemy text, an underline, a short impact entrance and directional exit above the tray. Other opening notices retain a separate panel style. TOTAL DAMAGE is a UIToolkit widget with a coral impact burst, outlined number, hit pulse and release fade. It accumulates calculated damage from every hit of one card/counter, including overkill after a recipient reaches zero HP, stays visible through long multi-hit actions, and resets even when the next card has the same owner. Large totals preserve every digit and saturate at `long.MaxValue`.

The ultimate first recovers the previous actor, then visibly moves from the previous camera state to its owner's overhead Rest destination, including after a support card. This explicit lead-in overrides normal support queue cuts. It hides hand/phase controls and plays the charge cue during travel and the subsequent arena-wide pause, then enters the portrait cut-in, actor WindUp and world reveal before rear Action. Rest holds throughout the overlay; there is no extra Rest detour before the actor reveal. Damage/target feedback stays visible during impact. Recovery restores the HUD. Skip/disable cancels travel, flags, overlay and world energy, restoring camera/actor/UI without changing gameplay costs or outcomes.

Starting values: ultimate Rest-camera travel 450 ms (`Ultimate Lead-In / Ultimate Rest Camera Duration`), then the existing 350 ms overhead pause. Travel finishes before the overlay appears. Playtest attack → ultimate → buff, team-heal → ultimate and consecutive ultimates on both sides. Pass if the pullback is visibly continuous, the larger action reads before its portrait, and Skip during travel leaves no delayed camera movement. If the return looks snapped, lengthen travel by 50 ms; if it drags, shorten it by 50 ms. If anticipation is missed, lengthen the pause by 50 ms; shorten it if consecutive ultimates stall the queue. Readability comes from wider face framing and deliberate Rest travel, satisfaction from charge/reveal cues, response from skippable cleanup. No balance or resource changes.

## Transitions and cleanup

- Rest enters WindUp on CardPlayed/CounterStarted for offensive cards and ranked support. Rank 1 support enters Action directly.
- WindUp exits after preparation feedback and an authored clip's completion, when present. Action begins the appropriate Attack/Debuff/Stance/Buff/Heal clip or procedural cast/strike.
- Action continues through all hit and after-damage barriers. AfterAction recovers the actor before after-action effects. ActionCompleted waits for remaining feedback; an empty current-turn card/counter queue returns to the drafting top view. Turn/status/battle boundaries terminate that queue even when later turns contain cards. A pending execution holds the current recovered shot (team view for non-self support) until the next actor's preparation.
- Recovery returns position, rotation and scale to formation. It waits for an authored Recovery clip when present, then enters Rest. Offensive camera follows home to its saved rear WindUp; self support cuts rear at Recovery entry; team support holds its team shot.
- Skip, restore and disable stop motion, reset actor phase/pose, unsubscribe the camera observer and restore Rest. Missing actors/animators and absent state clips use procedural feedback. Animation/motion watchdogs prevent broken clips from indefinitely blocking playback.
- Presentation phases consume no gameplay resources. Card costs and interruption/outcome decisions remain authoritative combat events.

## Animation integration

Optional Animator states or triggers: `WindUp`, `Action`, `Recovery`, `Idle`, plus category triggers `Attack`, `Debuff`, `Stance`, `Buff`, `Heal`. The component uses the category state/trigger first, then `Action`. Legacy sample controllers with no parameters reuse their `_skill1` and `_idle` states. Unauthored WindUp uses a visible anticipation lean/squash plus the existing charge cue; Action uses existing strike/cast feedback; Recovery supplies a settling motion. No new animation art is required for the phases to function. Existing `CardFirstHit`/`CardLastHit` markers continue synchronizing authored attacks; generated multi-hit templates retain their own timing.

Starting values: offensive Rank 1 preparation 180 ms; ranked preparation 650/850 ms; actor recovery 320 ms with a 2.5% fallback settling pulse. Offensive WindUp/Action/Rest entry uses 140/120/160 ms, with ranked face/reset and support cuts as specified above. Rear shoulder starts 0.35 model heights to the side, 1.3 above ground and 1.8 behind, then fits captured target/reaction geometry from the caster's current position. Close attacks open the angle toward 1.7 heights; size-based lateral clearance prevents foreground occlusion. Watchdogs retain 8 seconds. These are placeholder tuning values, not measured reference constants. Test alternating cards: anticipation and travel must be readable, impacts unobscured and Recovery complete before next preparation. Shorten anticipation if the queue drags; lengthen it if missed. Increase clearance if victims are hidden; reduce settling pulse if elastic.
