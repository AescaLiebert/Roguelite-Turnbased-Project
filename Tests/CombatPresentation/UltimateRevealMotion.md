# Ultimate reveal motion

The character art is an unrotated, ScaleToFit image directly under the full-screen overlay. It shows the complete supplied sprite, including sprites whose source artwork itself is a cropped portrait. Existing full-art references are preferred, followed by splash art, then icons. Transparent tint, diagonal ribbons and fine streaks keep the arena visible; no opaque black screen is introduced. Accent colors follow the fighter's attribute.

All motion values are **starting values**. Camera return: 0.22 seconds, with no idle pause on arrival. Opening: 0.28 seconds; hold: 0.72 seconds; exit: 0.42 seconds. The background arrives first, art follows, then kicker/title/name arrive individually with quintic easing. A small hold drift keeps the art alive. Text leaves first, character art accelerates out while fading, and the tint clears last. Tune by watching ten consecutive Ultimates: the name should be readable in one viewing, the art should remain completely framed at rest, and no stationary last frame should pop out. Increase hold if long names are unreadable; shorten hold if repeated casts slow the battle; increase exit if the return to the world feels abrupt.

State flow: enter after the existing overhead-camera anticipation; advance with unscaled time; remove the overlay on normal completion; return control to the existing Ultimate actor sequence. Skip, scene teardown and cancellation use the existing cinematic cleanup, including restoration of the execution-panel visibility. This cosmetic reveal consumes no additional resources and changes no combat state. Both teams use the same view.

Design checks: clarity comes from a readable move name and an uncropped image; response retains the existing skip path; satisfaction uses the existing Ultimate sound cue plus layered motion; fit uses authored character art and attribute color; motivation remains the pending Ultimate attack. There is no extra gameplay reward.

Visual fixture checks cover landscape and portrait layouts; hidden first frame; staggered opening; full-art frame; title bounds; transparent root; staggered exit; final zero opacity; both-team camera anticipation; repeat casts; and interruption cleanup. Captures sample the production timeline directly, so low frame rates cannot skip the opening/exit assertions.

Manual playtest: a new observer should identify who casts and read the move name; test the longest title and widest art; spam skip during opening, hold and exit; queue two Ultimates on both sides; verify no hidden execution panel persists; confirm full art remains inside landscape and portrait screens; confirm the existing sound cue and attack feedback are distinct. The source-art references can be replaced with complete full-body illustrations independently of this motion code.
